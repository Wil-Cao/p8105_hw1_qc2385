The mean of `norm_sample` works because it is numeric.

The mean of `greater_than_0` also works because R treats `TRUE` as 1 and
`FALSE` as 0;

The means of `char_var` and `factor_var` return `NA` because these
variables are not numeric or logical.

applies the as.numeric function:

``` r
as.numeric(hw1_df$greater_than_0)
as.numeric(hw1_df$char_var)
as.numeric(hw1_df$factor_var)
```

Converting the logical variable to numeric changes `TRUE` to 1 and
`FALSE` to 0. This explains why its mean gives the proportion of values
greater than zero.

The character values are letters, so they cannot be converted to numbers
and become NA.

Converting the factor gives the numeric codes of its levels (`high` = 1,
`low` = 2, and `medium` = 3). These codes are category labels, not
meaningful numerical measurements, so calculating their mean would not
be useful.

Therefore,`mean()` only works when a variable can be given a genuine
numeric interpretation.
